Learning is less about accumulating facts and more about changing how you see things.

The best moments in learning aren't when you get the right answer — they're when a question you thought was simple turns out to be deep. That slight disorientation, where your old mental model cracks open a little, is where growth actually happens.

A few things I keep coming back to:

- **Read slowly.** Skimming gives you the shape of an idea. Reading slowly gives you the texture.
- **Teach what you learn.** If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it yet.
- **Stay in the uncomfortable zone.** Competence feels good, but it's the edge of competence where learning lives.

There's a Chinese saying I like:

> 学而不思则罔，思而不学则殆。
> *Learning without thought is labor lost; thought without learning is perilous.*
> — Confucius

The balance between doing and reflecting is something I'm still figuring out. But I think the act of noticing — paying attention to what confuses you, what surprises you, what you assumed without realizing — is most of the work.

Stay curious.
